The Rise of Edge Computing

What it is | Why now

What it is

Edge computing moves computation away from centralized data centers and closer to where data is produced: factory floors, vehicles, phone masts and retail stores. Instead of shipping every raw event to a distant cloud, an edge node filters, aggregates and reacts to data locally.

Latency is the obvious winner. A round trip to a hyperscale data center typically costs 40-100 milliseconds; a local edge node answers in 1-5 milliseconds, which matters for robotics, AR and industrial control.

Why now

Three trends converged: cheap ARM-class hardware, mature container orchestration, and 5G radios that support local breakout. Analysts expect the edge market to grow from 60 billion USD in 2024 to over 130 billion USD by 2028, a compound annual growth rate of roughly 17%.

Bandwidth economics help too: a single wind turbine with 200 sensors can generate 2 TB per day, but only a few megabytes of that are decision relevant. Pre-processing at the edge cuts backhaul costs by up to 90%.
